# Product notes

**Who loses most?** Students preparing for JEE and NEET lose time, confidence and potentially marks when a broken question persists; a misleading answer can make a correct student doubt themselves. The queue groups repeated reports and puts unresolved cases first, so one faculty decision can help multiple students. The review screen makes the question, evidence, exact change and student-facing reply visible together. Student replies are previews, not messages sent by this prototype.

**Never auto-apply:** diagrams, newly authored distractors, syllabus or chapter decisions, and changes to fundamentally unsound questions. Answer keys also deserve especially strong independent verification; this prototype permits only two pinned, exact spelling corrections to auto-apply; other cases are reviewed by faculty. Taxonomy permission is an upper bound, not proof that a specific edit is safe.

**Week-one metric:** faculty-confirmed harmful change rate among applied fixes, measured from a sampled independent post-review audit, with the sample size and uncertainty reported. Track speed separately; fast but wrong fixes hurt students.

**Beyond the brief:** after a faculty-confirmed correction, identify affected attempts and offer a transparent re-evaluation or notification to students. This requires access to attempt history, authorisation, operational controls and a policy for prior scores; none exists in the sample.
